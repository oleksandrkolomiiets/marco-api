> "What's the difference between a bandeja and a vibora? When do I use each?"

Marco: Both shots are defensive-offensive overheads used when you can't smash outright — but they have different shapes and different moments.

The bandeja is your control shot. You slice across the ball with a flatter swing path, keeping it low and directed at a back corner. The goal isn't pace — it's to land it tight, stay at the net, and maintain your position. Use it when the lob is decent but not perfect, and you want to reset without giving your opponents an easy attack.

The víbora is more aggressive. You come over the ball with a windshield-wiper wrist snap, generating topspin and sidespin that makes it kick awkwardly off the glass. It's harder to control than the bandeja, but when you time it well it can genuinely hurt opponents. Use it when you have a bit more time and the lob is sitting up enough to attack — especially crosscourt on the left side, which is right in your wheelhouse.

The rule of thumb: when in doubt, bandeja. Keep the net, stay patient, wait for the real opportunity. The víbora is a weapon, not a default.

> "I keep getting caught flat-footed at the net. What should I do?"

Marco: Being flat-footed at the net is one of the most common problems at your level, and it's fixable.

The key is the split step. Just as your opponent is about to make contact with the ball, you do a small hop — both feet leave the ground briefly and land shoulder-width apart. That split second of movement loads your legs so you can push off in any direction instantly. If you're standing still and flat when they hit, you've already lost the reaction battle.

On the right side, you also want to stay slightly on your toes between points, weight forward, racket up. Not tense — just ready. Think of it like a goalkeeper before a penalty: alert, not rigid.

Given your last match frustration with missed volleys, I'd bet a lot of those came when the ball surprised you and you had no time to load. The split step alone can change that.

Try it in your next session — focus only on the split step timing for the first few points, even if your shots aren't perfect yet. Did you tend to get caught more on balls straight at you, or when your opponents went wide?
