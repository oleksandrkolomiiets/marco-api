Marco: Good question, Joost — and honestly, given what happened in your last match, working on your lob makes a lot of sense. Missing volleys at the net often means your opponents are lobbing over you, so having a solid lob yourself is a great weapon to have.

**The basics of a good lob:**

**1. Disguise it like a normal shot**
Don't telegraph it. Start your swing the same way you would for a regular groundstroke. The lob should be a surprise, not a signal.

**2. Open the racket face**
This is the key. Tilt the face slightly back at contact — that's what sends the ball up with height and gives it that soft, controlled arc.

**3. Swing low to high**
Think of brushing up the back of the ball. Smooth, controlled motion — not a panic swing. The more relaxed your arm, the better the result.

**4. Aim for depth**
A short lob is a gift to your opponent. Target the back third of the court, ideally the corners. Height + depth = very hard to smash.

**5. The backhand lob specifically**
Since that's one of your goals — on your backhand side, use two hands if that feels more stable. Focus on the brushing motion and don't rush it.

---

**Quick drill you can do:**
Have someone feed you balls at mid-court. Practice lifting them over an imaginary outstretched racket, landing in the back corners. Repetition is everything here.

> "What's the difference between a bandeja and a vibora? When do I use each?"

Marco: Great question, Sandra — and super relevant if you're working on your net game.

Both are overhead shots, but they do different jobs.

---

**Bandeja**
Think of it as your *control* overhead. You slice across the ball, keeping it low and directing it down the middle or at your opponents' feet. The goal isn't to win the point outright — it's to **stay at the net** and keep the pressure on. The ball stays in the court, your position stays strong.

Use it when:
- The lob isn't deep enough to smash but you don't want to retreat
- You want to keep your net position
- You need consistency over power

---

**Víbora**
This one's more aggressive. Similar swing path, but you brush the ball more sharply — almost like a side-spin whip — so it kicks off the glass unpredictably after the bounce. It's harder to control but much harder to defend.

Use it when:
- You want to finish a point or really push them back
- The ball sits up a bit more and you can commit to the shot
- You're confident in the contact

---

**The simple rule:**
Bandeja = stay in control, keep the net. Víbora = go for more, accept more risk.

Since your goal is getting sharper at the net, I'd start by owning your bandeja first. Once that's consistent, the víbora becomes a weapon. Vamos — what aspect do you want to dig into first?
