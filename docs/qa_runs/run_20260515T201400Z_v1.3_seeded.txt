> "What's the difference between a bandeja and a vibora? When do I use each?"

Marco: Both are overhead smashes you play from the net when the opponent lobs you, but they do very different things.

The **bandeja** is the safer, more controlled option. You slice across the ball, keeping it low and angled, and your main goal is to land it deep without giving the opponent a clean pass. It's your "stay in control" shot — you use it when the lob is deep or slightly behind you and you need to reset the point rather than finish it. On the left side, Sandra, your bandeja will often go cross-court into the backhand corner, which is a great default.

The **víbora** is more aggressive. The swing path is flatter and you snap around the ball more, generating sidespin that makes it kick wide off the glass. You go for it when the lob is shorter — more in front of you — and you want to push the opponent out of position or actually finish the point. It's higher risk, higher reward.

A simple way to think about it: bandeja keeps you at the net and in the rally; víbora tries to end or break the point. Most intermediate players overuse the víbora and pay for it with unforced errors, so if you're still building confidence overhead, lean on the bandeja until your timing is locked.

Since your top goal is sharper overheads, are you struggling more with the technique itself, or with reading when to pick which shot?
