> "What's the difference between a bandeja and a vibora? When do I use each?"

Marco: Both shots are defensive-offensive overhead options, but they work differently and suit different moments.

The **bandeja** is the safer of the two. You slice through the ball with a flat, controlled swing — the goal is to keep the ball low, central, and slow enough that you stay at the net after playing it. Think of it as a "reset" overhead: you're not winning the point with it, you're maintaining your net position and forcing your opponents to hit up again. It's your go-to when you're slightly off-balance or the lob isn't quite attackable.

The **vibora** is more aggressive. You brush the ball with a faster, sidespin-heavy swing — it kicks off the glass with a low, awkward bounce that's genuinely hard to read. You use it when you've got a cleaner lob to attack and you want to put real pressure on. The tradeoff is it demands better timing, and if you mistimed it you'll spray it wide or into the net.

Since your top goal is sharper overheads and you play the left side, you'll face lobs to your backhand corner a lot — that's typically where the bandeja becomes essential, because a vibora from deep on the backhand side is extremely difficult to execute. Nailing the bandeja first gives you the platform to add the vibora later.

Want to start with the mechanics of one of them specifically, or should I pull up a drill to work both into a session?

The habit you want to build is called the split step.
